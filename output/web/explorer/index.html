Six things the maps turned up

“Service runs until 9:50pm” is true and useless.

8 of 18 routes still run after 9pm. After 9:30pm, 3.

That figure is the last bus anywhere on the system, not anything a rider can plan around. Downtown — the best-served stop in Lincoln — reaches 0.8 sq mi at 9pm.

By 8pm, two of these stops have no bus at all.

Bryan East and Briarpark both read 0.5 sq mi.

That is about sixty grid cells, which is what a ten-minute walk covers. The map isn’t showing you a smaller bus network — it’s showing you walking. The last bus left Bryan East at 7:46pm and Briarpark at 6:26pm.

The worst time to catch a bus is the middle of the day.

Bryan East: 29 sq mi at 7am, 19 at 10am, 34 at 3pm.

Every origin does this — a dip from about 9am to 1pm that sits below the early morning, then a peak at 2–3pm. Dawn beats midday.

Two maps are pixel-for-pixel identical, and that’s correct.

Briarpark, weekday 8am and Briarpark, Saturday 5pm: 4.2 sq mi each.

Not similar — the same file, byte for byte. Route 56 is the only service there, and it runs hourly on both days. A 60-minute departure window over hourly service samples every possible wait — 0 through 59 minutes — exactly once, so the median can’t tell the two days apart. It looks like a bug and it’s a proof.

Saturday doesn’t taper off. It stops.

N 27th & Superior: 13 sq mi at 4pm, 8.6 at 5pm, 0.3 at 7pm.

Saturday holds one flat level all day rather than building to a peak, and then falls off a cliff inside three hours.

The schedule file claims 16 months and knows about one holiday.

Labor Day 2026. That’s the only exception in the feed.

It declares itself valid through the end of 2027, yet Thanksgiving, Christmas and the 4th of July are simply absent. Either StarTran runs an ordinary weekday on Christmas, or the feed only carries near-term exceptions — and nothing in the data says which.

One I can’t explain

I don’t know why the middle of the day is the worst time to travel. My guess is school-day service — StarTran runs it, and the afternoon peak lines up with a school letting out — but I haven’t checked that against the route timetables, so I’m not going to claim it.

One I got wrong

My first check of the routing network added up the legs of each trip. That made a two-bus ride to the Nebraska Union look like 8 minutes, and the check “failed.” The time spent standing at the origin before the first bus belongs to no leg, so it was being dropped. The network was right; my measurement was wrong. Every trip is now timed door to door, from when you leave to when you arrive.
